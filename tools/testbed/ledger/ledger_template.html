Verdict — is the mod at its goals?

The flat book proves the mechanism: for the first time the ladder is climbed on the anchor cadence, obsolete rungs shed workers, and construction runs at vanilla scale. What fails is the level — and the decomposition says how: the 1.56× GDP overshoot is 1.18× the productive workers × 1.33× the output per worker. The premise wanted the first factor below one; buildings this cheap absorb extra workers faster than the upgrade churn retires them. One dial — frontier cost up — plausibly closes both misses.

P — Performance: does our meddling make the game slower than vanilla?

A standing constraint on every batch, not a per-batch curiosity. Whatever the mod does to the economy, it must not make Victoria 3 materially slower to play than the base game. The budget is 10% overall. But it has to be measured at a comparable economy size: the engine's cost scales with how much it is simulating, so a mod whose world is half as developed — fewer distinct pops, a fraction of the GDP — finishes sooner while being slower per unit of work. That kind of "speedup" is not a result, it is the absence of an economy, and it barely counts. So the verdict is read pop-matched: seconds per in-game year compared between the arms at the same live pop-object count, never at the same date and never in total.

Incidents & confounds

  • n=1 by instruction. The comparison band is known-tight (the two ×1.5^era runs agreed within 3% on every headline), but country-level numbers are single-world observations.
  • The 1928–29 "depression" is two simultaneous state collapses, not a business cycle: Russia's civil war (pop 173M→17M at 1929, fully restored by 1931) and China fracturing into a Taiping state + seven warlord states. Individual vanilla runs carry the same event class (worst single-year world-GDP drawdowns −3.6…−12.0%), hidden by the n=4 average; this run's −16.3% is the largest observed but the same species.
  • One CTD at ~1908 — the 1.13.10 sway-recursion engine bug (F56), also seen on pure vanilla. Resume loaded clean and finished.
  • The port/railway subsidy bleed (F57 §5) is deliberately left in — constant across every compared arm.
  • All payback £ on the flat-£720/pt basis (F53: shared ~27% late-era bias).
  • The overshoot conflates "frontier too cheap" with "labour became the ceiling" — both present; n=1 cannot apportion.
