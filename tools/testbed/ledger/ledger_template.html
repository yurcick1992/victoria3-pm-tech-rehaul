P — Performance: does our meddling make the game slower than vanilla?

A standing constraint on every batch. Whatever the mod does to the economy, it must not make Victoria 3 materially slower to play. Graded on the total: ≤+5% acceptable, +5–15% needs improvement, >+15% too slow. Speed is reported as in-game years per minute — the rate you actually feel — so higher is better everywhere on this page.

Total run time is the quantity that matters — a player never notices that the economy half a world away carries more lightly-staffed buildings, they notice the century taking longer. So the aim is to keep the total down, and shrinking what we ask the engine to simulate is as legitimate a way to do that as making each unit cheaper.

The normalised views exist for a different job. ⚠ They must be read at a comparable economy size: an underdeveloped arm finishes sooner while being slower per unit of work, so the fair arm-vs-arm verdict is pop-matched. And ÷ modelled cost is a diagnostic tripwire: it should sit near 1.0 and near-flat, and if a build suddenly lifts or destabilises it, that build made each unit of world dearer — a fault that a change in economy size would otherwise mask.
